Alice's Café
Website Redesign

2025  ·  UX/UI  ·  Figma

A full redesign of the Alice's Village Café website — reworking information architecture, visual hierarchy, and brand identity to better serve both the café's customers and its personality.

The goal was to move from a cluttered, inconsistent layout to something warm, readable, and easy to navigate across all five pages.

Context

The Problem

The original Alice's Village Café site lacked visual consistency and made it difficult for users to find key information quickly. The redesign addressed both the UX structure and the visual language.

Before

Inconsistent typography and colour usage across pages. No clear visual hierarchy to guide users. Navigation felt disconnected and menus were hard to scan. The overall aesthetic didn't reflect the warmth of the café brand.

After

A unified design system with consistent type, spacing, and colour throughout. Clear page structure with obvious entry points for each content area. Navigation is simplified and prominent. Brand identity is warm, inviting, and cohesive.

Design Decisions

01

Visual Hierarchy First

Each page was structured so the most important content — the menu, hours, and location — is immediately visible without scrolling.

02

Consistent Type System

A unified typographic scale replaced the scattered font choices of the original, making every page feel like it belongs to the same family.

03

Brand-Aligned Colour

The palette was drawn from the café's existing identity — warm, earthy tones that feel welcoming rather than clinical or generic.

04

Simplified Navigation

Reduced the navigation to only the five essential pages, making it easier for users to orient themselves and find what they need.

05

Readable Menu Layout

Menu items were reorganised into scannable sections with clear pricing and descriptions, reducing the cognitive load of reading a long list.

06

Interactive Prototype

Built as a fully clickable Figma prototype to test the flow between pages and demonstrate how users would navigate the final site.
